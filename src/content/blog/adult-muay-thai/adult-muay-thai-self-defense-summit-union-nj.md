That's not a spinning back kick situation. That's a clinch situation. That's an elbow-and-knee-at-close-range situation. And that's the whole reason I've built our adult program around real Muay Thai instead of the highlight-reel version people see online.

## What Looks Good vs. What Works

I spent six years fighting professionally — IFL, Bellator, UWC where I won the light heavyweight title — and I can tell you the fights that actually got ugly were never decided by anybody's flying knee. They got decided in the clinch, in the scramble, in the two seconds where somebody grabbed onto you and you had to know what to do with your hands, your elbows, your hips.

Instagram rewards the pretty stuff. Cartwheel kicks, 540s, tornado spins. Cool to watch, useless if a guy who outweighs you by 40 pounds bull-rushes you in a tight space. Real Muay Thai — the stuff I learned coming up and still teach here — is built around three ranges: long range with the teep and low kick to keep people off you, mid-range boxing to control distance, and the clinch, where elbows and knees do the actual damage when things get close. Parking lots are close. Doorways are close. Most real altercations happen at a range where a spin kick would get you knocked out before you finished rotating.

## What a First Class Actually Looks Like

No one's throwing you into sparring on day one. You show up, we get you wrapped or gloved up, and we start with pad work — teeps, straight punches, low kicks — so you learn the tools before you're ever asked to use them against resistance. Then some clinch work against a partner, controlled, where you learn how to fight for position instead of just throwing knees wildly. That clinch work is the part most gyms skip because it's not flashy, and it's exactly the part that matters if somebody actually grabs you.

I've got professionals from Summit who come straight from the train after work, guys from Union on their lunch break, a few Millburn dads who train while their kids are at travel practice. Nobody's there to look cool. They're there because it works and because the room doesn't care what you looked like before you walked in.
