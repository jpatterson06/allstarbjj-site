## The Question I Get Every August

A guy from Livingston called me last week. He'd just watched a "self-defense demo" at some strip-mall school off Route 10, and something felt off. Everyone was bowing a lot. Nobody was sweating. He asked me straight: "How do I know if a BJJ school is legit before I hand over a credit card?"

Good question. Livingston Rec's Fall 2026 Adult & Senior registration opened August 19. Schools go back in session right after Labor Day. Every gym in Essex and Union County is about to hit you with a fall enrollment push, and half of them are selling you a costume, not a skill.

Here's how you actually check.

## Ask Who Promoted the Instructor — And Get a Real Answer

This is the first filter, and most people skip it because it feels rude to ask. It's not rude. It's the single most important question you can ask a jiu-jitsu instructor.

I'm a black belt under Renzo Gracie. That's not a marketing line — it's a lineage you can trace. Renzo comes from Carlos Gracie Sr. through Helio Gracie's line, decades of documented promotion, real competition, real accountability. When I promote one of my guys to blue, purple, brown, or black at All Star in Union, that belt means something because my belt means something.

A McDojo instructor will get vague here. "I trained under a few different guys." "I have a 5th degree black belt in mixed martial arts." That's not a lineage, that's a resume with no references. Ask the follow-up: how many years did you train under that black belt before you got promoted? If the answer is under six or seven years, or they can't name a specific instructor and specific gym, keep walking.

I fought pro for years — IFL, Bellator, UWC, where I won the light heavyweight title — before I ever opened a school. That record isn't a flex for the website. It's proof I got tested against resisting opponents who were trying to hurt me, under real rules, for real stakes. That's the same standard I hold the mat to every single day at All Star.

## Watch One Class. Count How Much Live Rolling Happens.

This is the tell that separates everybody, instantly.

Go watch a class — any class, any level — and count the minutes of live sparring versus choreographed drilling. In a real jiu-jitsu room, live rolling happens every single class. White belts roll live from week one, at appropriate intensity, with control from higher belts. That's how the art works. You can't learn to swim by watching someone describe swimming.

A McDojo hides from this. They'll do "self-defense scenarios" where the attacker grabs your wrist and stands there. They'll do board breaking. They'll do elaborate belt-testing ceremonies with a $150 testing fee attached to every stripe. What they won't do is put two resisting humans on the mat and let one try to submit the other. Because live resistance training exposes fake technique immediately. Fake technique doesn't work when someone's actually trying to stop you.

At All Star, adults roll live every class. Beginners roll live their first week, paired smart, coached hard. If a gym in Livingston or Millburn tells you they "ease into sparring after a few months," ask why. There's no good answer.

## Belt Testing Fees Are a Red Flag, Not a Tradition

Related point: if a school charges you separately every time you get a stripe or a belt, on top of your monthly tuition, that's a business model built around slowing down your progress to maximize billing cycles. Real BJJ lineages promote you when you're ready, included in what you're already paying. I've promoted guys from Kenilworth and Springfield who trained hard for years — the belt was never a separate invoice.

## Try Before You Buy — Actually Do It

Don't sign a 12-month contract off one conversation with a sales guy in a polo shirt. Any legitimate gym will let you train a real class, roll if you're ready, and talk to current students without a hard sell standing over your shoulder.

When people drive over from Livingston, Cranford, Westfield, or Summit to check us out, I tell them the same thing: come to a class, not a tour. Tours show you a clean mat and a trophy case. Classes show you whether the black belts actually teach beginners, whether the room is intense but safe, and whether you can survive round one without getting smashed by a purple belt who has something to prove. Our room isn't like that. We've got guys in their 40s who started with zero grappling experience training next to competitors, and it works because everyone rolls at the right speed for where they're at.
