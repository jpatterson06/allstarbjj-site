## It's Late October. You Said You'd Start "When Things Calm Down."

I had this conversation three times last week alone. Guy drives home from Summit down Springfield Ave, past our building, tells himself November is a mess with the holidays coming, he'll start in January when work settles and the kids' schedules even out.

It won't settle. It never does. I'm 41 years old running a gym — I know exactly how this goes. The people who show up tonight are training by Thanksgiving. The people waiting for calm are still waiting next October.

So let's deal with the actual reasons you haven't started, because they're real, and I've built this program around them for fifteen years.

## Your Schedule Doesn't Care About Your Work Schedule — Ours Does

Most of our Adult BJJ guys are commuting home off Route 24 or coming from the train station in Summit, and they're walking into the gym in Union still half in work mode. That's why our evening classes run in two blocks — one right after the typical workday ends, one later for the people who need to grab dinner with the family first or finish a call.

I didn't design that schedule from a textbook. I designed it because I used to be the guy stuck in traffic trying to make a 6pm class from Springfield with my gi bag thrown in the back seat. If the schedule doesn't survive contact with a real commute, it's useless. Ours does.

## Old Shoulder. Bad Knee. Fifteen Extra Pounds. Zero Flexibility. Fine.

Every single person reading this has some version of a physical excuse. A shoulder that clicks. A knee from high school football that never fully came back. Fifteen pounds that crept on over the last two years of sitting at a desk. I'm not going to tell you that's not real.

Here's what actually happens in class: drilling and rolling intensity scale to the person, not the other way around. If you've got a bad knee, we're not putting you in a leg lock exchange on day three. If your shoulder doesn't like certain positions, I know the modifications because I've had the same injuries from six years of pro fights — IFL, Bellator, I won a UWC light heavyweight title with a torn labrum I didn't get fixed until after I retired. I'm not coaching from a textbook here either.

This is a Renzo Gracie lineage school. The entire system is built on using leverage and technique instead of strength and athleticism — which means it was literally designed for the 35-year-old who isn't in fighting shape, not the 22-year-old college wrestler. The sport favors the person training around limitations, not the person ignoring them.

## You Will Be the Least Fit Person in the Room on Day One. So Was Everyone Else.

I want to be direct about this because it's the thing that keeps people in Summit and Union sitting on the sidelines for years: yes, on your first night, there's a good chance you'll be gassed in four minutes, you'll tap more than you'd like, and you'll watch a 50-year-old guy with bad knees move better than you expect.

That guy was exactly where you are two years ago. Everyone on our mats walked in out of shape, stiff, and self-conscious about it. Nobody in that room is thinking about your conditioning — they're thinking about their own first class, because they remember it clearly.

The gas tank and the flexibility come from showing up, not from having them before you show up. That's the actual order of operations, and people get it backwards for years before they finally walk in.

## What Actually Happens Your First Week

No gi required to start. No experience required, period. You show up in a t-shirt and shorts, I pair you with a training partner at your level, and you learn two or three positions that first class — usually something simple like escaping bottom mount or securing a basic guard pass. You'll drill it slowly, then roll light if you want to, or just drill if you'd rather skip live rolling your first night. Both are fine. I'd rather build you correctly over three weeks than impress you for one.

Guys coming from Summit, Cranford, Westfield, Millburn — it's the same room, same instruction, same pace. Nobody's getting fast-tracked or babied based on zip code. You get coached the way the person next to you gets coached: based on what your body can do right now.
