## The Tuesday After Labor Day

I had four guys walk into the 6:15pm class last week and tell me the same thing in different words: "I haven't worked out since June." Kids were on summer schedules, work got weird, the gym membership sat unused. Now it's September, the buses are running again, and everybody's kid has a routine except them.

Here's what I tell them. Back-to-school isn't just for the kids. You get a reset too. The difference is nobody's making you use it.

I'm Jamal Patterson. I run All Star Martial Arts in Union, and a big chunk of our adult roster drives over from Mountainside — Route 22 makes it a straight shot, maybe twelve minutes from the center of town. I fought pro MMA for years — IFL, Bellator, UWC light heavyweight title — and I'm a Renzo Gracie black belt. None of that matters to you on your first night. What matters is whether this fits into a week that already doesn't have room in it.

## The Schedule Problem Is Real, So We Built Around It

Most adults who walk in here aren't avoiding training because they don't want it. They're avoiding it because their calendar is a mess. Drop-off at 7:45, work until 5:30, dinner needs to happen, homework needs checking. Somewhere in there, is there an hour for you?

We run early morning classes before the workday starts and evening sessions after most people are home from Mountainside, Kenilworth, Springfield — wherever you're coming from. The 6:15pm class exists specifically so you can do drop-off, put in a full day, and still make it home in time to sit down with your family. We're not asking you to blow up your week. We're asking for one hour, a few times, that's just yours.

I've had guys tell me the drive from Mountainside is actually the best part — fifteen minutes in the car where nobody needs anything from them, then an hour where the only job is to move and breathe and not think about the inbox. That's not a bonus feature. That's the point.

## Your Knee Doesn't Scare Us

Second biggest reason people stall out before they start: "I threw my back out in 2019" or "my knee's been bad since high school football" or "I had shoulder surgery, I don't know what I can even do."

Every drill in this room gets scaled. That's not a slogan, that's how I coach. If takedowns bother your knee, we adjust your base and change the entry. If your shoulder doesn't like overhead movement, we change the grip or skip that rep entirely. Nobody here is chasing a rep count at the cost of your joints. I've been hurt enough times in a pro career to know the difference between training through discomfort and training through actual damage, and I'm not going to let you make that mistake because you're embarrassed to say something hurts.

When you walk in for your first class, tell the coach what's going on with your body before you tell them your name. Bad knees, old shoulder surgery, a back that acts up when it rains — we need to know so we can modify, not so we can judge. Nobody's handing out a trophy for pushing through pain in this gym. That's not toughness, that's just a bad decision that costs you six weeks on the couch.

## You Will Not Be the Least Fit Person Here — And If You Are, So What

This is the one that keeps people out of the building the longest. The idea that everyone else is going to be some shredded 25-year-old who's been training for five years, and you're going to show up gasping for air on minute two.

Here's the truth: most of the adults in our evening classes started exactly where you're starting. Winded fast. Coordination a little rusty. Forty years old and it's been a decade since they threw a real punch or changed levels for a takedown. Nobody in that room is comparing you to the guy who's been here since 2019. Coaching is paced to you — if you need thirty extra seconds between rounds while everyone else keeps rolling, you take it. If a drill needs to be slower and cleaner before it needs to be faster, we slow it down.

The five-year guy isn't looking at you and judging your gas tank. He remembers his first class. Everybody in this room does.

## What Actually Happens Your First Night

You show up, you tell the coach about any injuries, you get a rash guard or wear a t-shirt, and you walk through basics — stance, movement, one or two techniques broken down slow. Then some light drilling at a pace you control. Nobody's throwing a brand-new person into hard sparring on day one. That's not how good gyms operate, and it's definitely not how mine operates.

You'll sweat. You'll probably be sore in muscles you forgot you had. You'll also leave feeling like you did something for yourself instead of for your inbox or your kid's carpool schedule.
