## The Treadmill You Bought in January Is Now a Coat Rack

I know because I hear about it every summer. Guys and women from Kenilworth, Union, Springfield — they come in for a trial class and tell me the same story. Bought the membership. Went hard for three weeks. Then it got hot, work got busy, and the treadmill turned into the place you throw your laundry. Nobody guilted them into going back. Nobody even noticed they stopped.

That's the actual problem with a regular gym. Not the equipment. The silence. You can disappear from a Planet Fitness for six months and the only thing that changes is your card gets declined for lapsed payment. Nobody at that gym knows your name.

## Somebody Notices When You're Not on the Mat

We run adult MMA classes at All Star five, six days a week, and here's what happens when a regular misses two classes in a row: I text them. So does the guy he drills with on Tuesdays. So does the woman from Kenilworth who always partners with him for takedown reps because they're the same size.

That's not me being a hardass coach. That's just how it works when you train with the same 15-20 people three times a week. You're not anonymous. You show up, or your training partners feel it, and you feel that they felt it. I've had students tell me the only reason they didn't skip a Thursday class after a bad week at work is because they knew their rolling partner was expecting them. That's accountability a machine can't manufacture.

## You Can Actually See Yourself Getting Better

A treadmill gives you a number. Miles, calories, minutes. It doesn't mean anything after week four because your body adapts and the number stops moving in any direction that feels like progress.

MMA gives you stripes on your belt, and it gives you sparring rounds where you can feel, physically, that the same over-hook that used to smash you doesn't work on you anymore. I've had 35-year-olds who hadn't done a single athletic thing since high school football walk in soft and gassed, and four months later they're stuffing takedowns from guys half their age. That's not a compliment I hand out for free — I watched Renzo Gracie build his reputation on that kind of unglamorous, honest progress, and it's the same standard we hold here in Union. You bank mat time, you get better. Nobody has to tell you. You feel it in live rounds.

Summer's actually the best window for this. Fall brings back school schedules, work travel picks up, and a lot of guys tell me they wish they'd banked more hours in July and August instead of promising themselves "I'll get serious in September." September you is going to be busier, not less busy.

## Every Class Is Different, Which Is the Whole Point

Here's the other thing about that gym membership you let lapse — the workout was the same. Same three machines, same treadmill, maybe a class you got bored of by week six because the choreography never changed.

Our adult MMA program rotates striking days and grappling days, and inside grappling days you're drilling something different depending on where the class is in the curriculum — guard passing one week, wrestling takedowns the next, back takes after that. Then live rounds, where your partner is never doing the exact same thing twice because they're a person reacting to you, not a machine running the same program. Some nights you're paired with a Kenilworth firefighter built like a truck. Other nights it's a 140-pound blue belt who submits you with pure technique. You don't get bored because the puzzle in front of you changes every single class.

## What Actually Happens Your First Day

If you've never trained, here's the honest version, no surprises: you show up, we get you a gi or rashguard if you don't have one, and you drill basics with a partner at your pace. Nobody throws a nervous 35-year-old with two left feet into a live spar on day one. I built this program around the idea that your first class should make you want a second one, not make you sore enough to quit. I've been running this room since 2011, I fought professionally for years before that — IFL, Bellator, won a UWC light heavyweight title — and I coach it the same way my instructors trained me under the Renzo Gracie banner: hard work, real technique, no ego.
